Neutron Shielding Monte Carlo

Thermal neutrons hit a stack of shielding layers at normal incidence. Each neutron is followed until it is reflected (x < 0), transmitted (beyond the last layer) or absorbed.

Physical model

  • A mono-energetic beam of thermal neutrons enters the shield at x = 0, moving in +x.
  • Layers are infinite slabs in y and z, stacked along x.
  • The distance to the next collision is exponential with mean free path λ = 1/Σt, where Σ = ρ NA σ / M is the macroscopic cross-section.
  • At a collision the neutron is absorbed with probability Σa/Σt, otherwise it scatters isotropically with no energy loss (one-speed model).
  • A neutron is reflected when x < 0 and transmitted when it passes the last layer.

Woodcock (delta) tracking

Flight distances are sampled with the largest cross-section in the stack, Σmax. At a collision point in a layer with Σt, the collision is real with probability Σt/Σmax; otherwise it is fictitious and the neutron continues in the same direction. This gives exact results without having to find where each flight crosses a layer boundary. For a single layer every collision is real.

Limitations

  • No energy dependence: thermal cross-sections are used throughout, so moderation and energy-dependent absorption (for example 1/v absorbers) are not modelled.
  • Scattering is isotropic in the laboratory frame.
  • Cross-sections for hydrogenous materials depend on chemical binding; the library values are approximate.
